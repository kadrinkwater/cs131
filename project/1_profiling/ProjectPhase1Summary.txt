Data Schema is basic TSV with four columns.
	prev: The previous page the user came from (either its Wikipedia title, or a category[*])
	curr: The title of the page being viewed.
	type: 
		link (if following a link from one Wikipedia page to another)
		external (if the referrer is not en(.m)?.wikipedia.org)
		other (prev and curr are both Wikipedia articles, but prev does not link to curr; this usually means people went to the search bar)
	count: Number of times this (prev, curr) pair occured in the given month.

The ordering of the lines seems weird and inconsistent, but this must be a result of how the page view data is collected and collated into these counts.



USING CUT, SORT, UNIQ TO LOOK AT TOP REFERRERS

time cut -f 1 2026-06.tsv > cut06
9.9s

time sort cut06 | uniq -c | sort -nr > referrers-ranked.txt
34.9s / referrers-ranked.txt is 59M and 2 million lines

I took the top 200 referrers and categorized them by hand.
By far the greatest number come from other-empty, other-search, other-internal, and other-external, in that order. Then we get Main_Page. Then it starts with lists: "Deaths in 2026" is, depressingly, first. A lot of lists are very high in the rankings. The first non-list actual page is "2026 FIFA World Cup". As we go down, we see lists getting more obscure ("List of nu metal bands") and more non-list pages coming in. The highest ranked individual person is Michael Jackson.



LOOKING AT TRAFFIC THROUGH A SINGLE PAGE

Let's look at the page for "San Francisco Bay Area".
time grep "\bSan_Francisco_Bay_Area\b" 2026-06.tsv > sfba.txt
1.3s to grep through 1 month of data

grep "^San_Francisco_Bay_Area" 2026-06.tsv > from-sfba.txt
grep -e "\sSan_Francisco_Bay_Area\s" 2026-06.tsv > to-sfba.txt
awk '{print $4 " " $1}' to-sfba.txt | sort -nr > sfba-incoming.txt
awk '{print $4 " " $2}' from-sfba.txt | sort -nr > sfba-outgoing.txt

Requests for pages that get redirected were mapped to the page they redirect to. Finally, any `(referrer, resource)` pair with 10 or fewer observations was removed from the dataset.
